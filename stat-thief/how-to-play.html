How to play Stat Thief

A complete walkthrough of a draft, including the rules the interface only half-explains.

Guide · about 6 minutes to read · updated November 2026

Stat Thief is a drafting game with one unusual rule: you never keep a player. You keep one attribute from each player you draw, and after ten rounds those ten fragments are welded into a single fictional footballer. Everything else about a run - the clubs, the respins, the random events, the season that follows - exists to make those ten choices interesting.

This guide walks through a draft in order.

Step 1: pick a position

Before anything spins you choose Forward, Midfielder or Defender. This is the most consequential decision in the game and it is made before you have any information, which feels unfair the first time and stops feeling unfair once you understand what it does.

Your position choice does two things at once:

  • It filters the pool. Only players whose real role matches your choice can ever appear in the draw. Pick Defender and you will never be offered a striker's finishing.
  • It sets the maths. Each position weighs the eight attributes differently when the final overall rating is calculated. Tackling is worth more than twice as much to a defender as passing is; for a midfielder that relationship is reversed.

The attributes are even renamed per position. The same underlying stat is called Finishing for a forward, Shooting for a midfielder and, for a defender, still Shooting but worth almost nothing. The full table is in Choosing your position.

Step 2: the two reels

Each round spins twice. First a club from Premier League history, then a player from that club who fits your position. The two-stage draw is not decoration: it is what makes respins meaningful, because you get to react to the club before you know the player, and to the player before you commit.

The game will not hand you the same club twice in a row, and it will not hand you the same player twice in a row, so a draft always keeps moving.

Step 3: steal one attribute

When a player lands, ten things appear: eight attributes graded on an S-to-F scale, one unique playstyle trait - things like Poacher, Visionary Playmaker, Defensive Titan, Speed Demon - and the player's nationality.

You take exactly one of the ten. Everything else about that player is discarded permanently. This is the core tension of the game: a player can be excellent in four areas and you still only leave with one of them.

Nationality is the odd one out: on its own it adds nothing to the overall rating. It pays off through the national bond: every other steal you made from a player of that country adds to a bonus of up to +3. Steal England after taking three attributes from English players and the flag is worth more than most single grades.

Two rules about stealing that are easy to miss:

  • An unfilled attribute is not zero. Anything you never steal is treated as a mediocre baseline rather than nothing, so a build with holes is bad but not catastrophic.
  • Stealing the same attribute twice replaces rather than stacks. A second, better Pace overwrites the first and is labelled as an upgrade. A second, equal or worse one is locked, so you can never waste a round on it.

Playstyle traits are not cosmetic

A trait occupies your one steal for the round, and in exchange it applies permanent bonuses to two or three specific attributes for the rest of the run. A Defensive Titan adds heavily to tackling and meaningfully to power and aerial ability. A Visionary Playmaker does the same for passing, with smaller bonuses to dribbling and mental. In weighting terms a well-chosen trait is often worth more than a single S-grade attribute, because it touches several of the stats your position actually cares about. See Draft strategy for when a trait beats a grade.

Step 4: respins

You begin every draft with one club respin and one player respin. They are separate currencies and they behave differently:

  • A club respin throws away the whole club and draws a new one, which also draws a new player. Use it when a club's eligible players are all wrong for your build.
  • A player respin keeps the club and draws a different player from it. Use it when the club is strong but the specific name is not.

Random events can grant extra respins, so the number you start with is a floor rather than a cap.

Step 5: random events

From round two onwards an event may fire at the start of a round, before you spin. It is announced on screen, so you know what you are spinning into. There are six, and they split evenly into three good and three bad:

EventEffect
Hot StreakThe next player's stats are boosted by +5
Magic NightThe next player's stats are boosted by +8
Generous ScoutYou gain an extra club respin
Rainy DayThe next player's stats drop by 3
HeadwindThe next player's stats drop by 5
Back DoorYou gain an extra player respin

The stat modifiers apply to the next draw only, which makes them far more strategic than they look. A Magic Night turns an ordinary player into a source of an inflated attribute worth locking in; a Headwind is the moment to steal something you were always going to take at a low grade anyway, or to burn a respin you were saving.

Step 6: name your legend

After the tenth steal the draft closes and you name the player you just built. The name carries through every screen that follows - the squad, the league table, the Champions League run, the Ballon d'Or ceremony - so it is worth two seconds of thought.

Step 7: the overall rating and the tier

The finished build is scored by combining your eight final attributes using your position's weights, then adding any playstyle bonuses. That single number is your OVR, and it drops you into a tier:

OVRTier
91+Legendary Icon
85-90Ballon d'Or Nominee
78-84Top 6 Material
70-77PL Starter
60-69Championship Battler
under 60Sunday League

A first draft usually lands somewhere in the low seventies. Consistently reaching the mid eighties means you have understood the weightings; breaking 91 needs the weightings, a well-timed trait and a little luck from the events.

Step 8: what happens next

A finished legend does not sit still. You choose a club, play out a league season, attempt the Champions League, find out whether the Ballon d'Or ceremony calls your name, then move into an off-season of training, lifestyle decisions and transfer offers before running it all again with the same player. That whole second half of the game is covered in Seasons and career mode.

Five things new players get wrong

  1. Stealing the highest grade on screen instead of the highest grade their position weighs.
  2. Hoarding both respins until round nine, when there is nothing left to fix.
  3. Ignoring playstyle traits entirely for a whole run.
  4. Ignoring bonds: three steals from one club, or a nationality backed by other steals, are worth up to +3 each.
  5. Choosing a position by favourite player rather than by which attributes they enjoy chasing.
