Seasons & career mode

What happens to your legend after the tenth steal: clubs, league tables, Europe, the Ballon d'Or and the off-season economy.

Guide · about 6 minutes to read · updated November 2026

The draft is half the game. Once your legend has a name and an overall rating, Stat Thief hands them a career: a club, a league season, a European campaign, an awards night, and an off-season in which the player you built keeps changing. This guide covers that second half.

Choosing a club

After naming your player you pick where they sign. Clubs differ in strength, and strength affects almost everything downstream: where you finish in the league, whether you qualify for the Champions League, how likely an award nomination becomes, and - counter-intuitively - what you get paid.

The wage model deliberately inverts the obvious. Less prestigious clubs offer higher base wages, because they have to overpay to attract a player of your quality, while elite clubs pay less in base terms and give you a far better chance of trophies and nominations. Your rating adds a bonus on top, and your squad status multiplies the whole thing: a star player earns roughly 30% more than a key player, and a squad player earns about 30% less.

So the club decision is a genuine trade-off rather than a formality. Join a giant and chase the Ballon d'Or; join a mid-table side, be the best player there by a distance, and bank money for the off-season upgrades below.

The league season

The season simulation runs the full table and places your club in it, weighting your own rating into the result. A 91-rated player at a mid-table club genuinely drags them up the table; a 72-rated player at a giant mostly gets carried. Your final league position feeds directly into whether you play European football and how the awards see you.

Big games and the Champions League

Individual fixtures are surfaced as one-off Big Game moments rather than simulated ninety minutes at a time, which keeps a season to a couple of minutes. The Champions League runs as a knockout: qualify through your league finish, then play the rounds out one at a time. Deep runs in Europe are the single biggest lever on your reputation, which matters for what comes next.

The Ballon d'Or ceremony

At the end of each season the ceremony asks whether your legend is nominated. Three things decide it: your overall rating, your league finish, and how far you went in Europe. The rating is the entry ticket - the tier named Ballon d'Or Nominee starts at 85 for a reason - but a high rating at a struggling club will not be enough on its own, and a slightly lower rating on a title-winning, European-finalist side often is.

This is where the club decision pays off or does not, and it is the reason the last two rounds of a draft matter more than their raw point value suggests: crossing 85 changes what the ceremony can do with you.

The off-season

Between seasons your player is not frozen. Three systems run.

Stat evolution

Attributes progress and decline. Young players improve; past a certain age, physical attributes start going the other way, which is exactly the point at which a build that leaned entirely on pace and power discovers the cost of that choice. A build weighted towards passing, vision or leadership ages considerably better - something worth knowing during the draft if you intend to play several seasons rather than one.

Spending the summer

You spend the money you earned in three areas. Training: rest costs nothing and does nothing, an elite camp adds +2 to the attribute you choose, and a world-class camp adds +3 to it and +1 to your next most important one. Recovery: a private medical team lowers your injury risk for the coming season, which matters more every year after 29. Profile: a media tour or a brand launch raises your fame, and fame raises both your wages and your brand income. Spending everything on training and nothing on recovery is a recognisable trap, and the game lets you fall into it.

Social and lifestyle choices feed your fame, which in turn multiplies future wages. Fame is not vanity: a famous player is offered materially better contracts.

The transfer window

Offers arrive based on your rating and your age. Fees scale steeply at the top - a 90-rated player commands several times what a 75-rated one does - and are discounted sharply once a player passes 28, at roughly 12% per year beyond it. Moving means new wages, a new squad status, and a new set of realistic ambitions for the season ahead.

Playing multiple seasons

Each completed season loops back into the next with the same legend, so a career is a slow accumulation: the build you drafted in eight minutes gets sharpened, aged, paid, transferred and occasionally decorated. The career summary at the end lays out what actually happened across all of it.

A career in progress lasts as long as your tab does, so finish one you care about in one sitting. When you retire, the career is scored and saved to the Hall of Fame on the home screen, stored in your own browser. You can retire at any point from age 30, and every career ends at 36.

Three career-mode habits worth having

  1. If your rating is below 85, sign for a strong club - you need the league finish and the European run to compensate.
  2. If your rating is above 85, consider a mid-table club for one season: the wage premium funds off-season upgrades that carry you for the rest of the career.
  3. Spend at least something on recovery every off-season. An injury costs more than the training bonus you bought instead.
